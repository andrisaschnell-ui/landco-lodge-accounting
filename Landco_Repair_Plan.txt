1. Business Context and Purpose
This section documents the business reality of Landco Lodge as understood from the 'Lanco explained' document and the supplementary 'Update the page with Andrisa' document. A correct understanding of the business is essential before any technical repair work is attempted.
1.1 Ownership Structure
Landco Lodge Lda is a property and hospitality group located in Vilankulo, Mozambique. The company is owned by four private individuals — referred to in the system as H1, H2, H3, and H4. Each owner holds a private home on the shared property and is linked to a specific named house:

A fifth property code, NEGU, represents the shared boat asset co-owned by H1 and H4. There is also a category called 'Com' (Communal) covering shared infrastructure, communal workers, and shared costs such as electricity, taxes, vehicles, and general maintenance.
1.2 Income Reality vs. Official Income
The real income of Landco consists entirely of cash injections from the four house owners. These funds enter the accounting system in several ways:
• Cash deposits into official bank accounts (MZN or USD)
• Black-market currency conversion from USD to MZN, followed by deposit as petty cash or bank credit
• Direct cash inputs to the petty cash box

Because Mozambican tax law would classify all such owner contributions as taxable income if no other income source is demonstrated, the company generates invoices for accommodation and boat-trip rental. These invoices are 'dummy' invoices — their purpose is to satisfy the official accountant BDO and the Mozambican Receiver of Revenue (Autoridade Tributária de Moçambique, AT) by presenting a tourism-based income stream. IVA (VAT) shown on these invoices is then offset against IVA on official supplier expenses, with the balance paid to the AT.
IMPORTANT — Dual Accounting System:
The application maintains two parallel accounting realities. The 'official' books show income from invoices and record only expenditures with official supplier documentation. The 'unofficial' books capture the true flow of owner funds into and out of the system, including cash expenses that cannot be supported by official supplier invoices (local materials, temporary labour, fuel for boats, imported spare parts, informal food suppliers). This report designs the application to support both realities clearly and separately.
1.3 Expense Classification
Expenses fall into two broad categories:
• Official expenses: Documented with registered supplier invoices; processed through the Expense Payments module; submitted to BDO for official accounting. IVA on these is recoverable.
• Unofficial (informal) expenses: Paid in cash (petty cash or informal cash); no official invoice available; not recoverable for IVA purposes; tracked in the system for owner reporting only, not for the AT.

Employees are split between those assigned to a specific house (H1–H4) and communal staff (guards, manager, gardener) assigned to the property as a whole. Salaries, advances, and statutory payments (INSS employer/employee contributions and IRPS income tax) are formally processed via BDO each month.
1.4 Reporting Objectives
The application must produce two categories of output:
• BDO Output Files: Monthly data formatted for the official accountant, covering income (dummy invoices), documented expense payments, payroll, bank movements. These must reconcile with the AT submissions.
• Owner Reports: Per-owner (H1–H4) monthly statements showing opening balance, money contributed (deposits), money spent (communal and personal expenses), and closing balance. These reports are private and not submitted to any authority.

Additionally, a comparison report must show the difference between dummy invoice totals and real owner-contributed income, so management can verify that the invoicing strategy is functioning as intended.

2.5 Known Problems Identified
The following issues have been identified from direct analysis of the source code and documentation:
2.5.1 Income Source Error — Critical
The most serious accounting error in the current system: income_transactions are currently populated using the dummy invoice system. Financial statements (P&L, shareholder balances) therefore report fabricated income figures. The true income source — owner cash deposits — has been identified in the Excel 'INCOME' sheets of the monthly 'MONTH END' files, but these have never been correctly parsed and loaded into a dedicated table. This error propagates to all P&L and shareholder balance calculations.
2.5.2 Exchange Rate Not Editable on Dashboard
The Exchange Rate field displayed on the Dashboard landing page is not connected to an editable data source. Users cannot update the MZN/USD rate from the dashboard. The Settings and Properties pages should also allow rate management per year. This means all USD/MZN conversions displayed in the application are stale or incorrect.
2.5.3 Expense Payments Page — Incomplete
Per the Andrisa requirements document, the Expense Payments page is missing several required fields and behaviours: 'Amount' label still shows '(MZN)' suffix; Expense Category is not linked to Account Mapping; Expense Target (H1/H2/H3/H4/Com/Negu) is missing; Payment Source dropdown linking to cash and bank accounts is missing; Supplier field is missing; the Recent Expense Payments list lacks a period filter, has incorrect column headings, and lacks a totals row.
2.5.4 Dual-Mode Confusion (Supabase Cloud vs Local)
The codebase contains references to both Supabase Cloud (neakxehuonsrlvjrxhnd.supabase.co) and the local Docker PostgreSQL stack. The sync mechanism between cloud and local has had documented sync failures (FIX_SYNC.md). The current live environment is local Docker, but the code still carries Supabase client configuration pointing at the cloud. This creates a risk of data being written to the wrong backend.
2.5.5 Journal Entry Backfill Not Confirmed
Release 1 introduced auto-posting triggers for double-entry bookkeeping. However, all data imported before those triggers were applied does not have corresponding journal entries. The backfill script (scripts/backfill-journal.mjs) must be run against the local database, but it is not confirmed whether this has been done. The Trial Balance will be incorrect until this is verified.
2.5.6 Import Accuracy Unverified
The parsers for Month End, Petty Cash, BDO Bank, Salary, and BIM files have been written but their accuracy against the actual Excel source files has not been systematically verified. January–March 2026 data may contain parsing errors, mismatched employee names, wrong category assignments, or missed rows.
2.5.7 Sources / Cash Account Dashboard Missing
The Andrisa document specifies a 'Sources' dashboard giving access to four financial account pages: Cash Box, Cheque Account (MZN), Cheque Account (USD), Debit Card (MZN). This dashboard does not yet exist. Without it, the link between payment sources and expense payments cannot be tracked or balanced.
2.5.8 Landco Income Page Missing
A dedicated 'Landco Income' page showing the real owner-contributed income from the MONTH END Excel INCOME sheets does not yet exist. This page is required to make the true income visible, editable, and integrated into all financial calculations.
2.5.9 All Screens Read-Only
Outside of the Upload and Settings pages, the application has no CRUD capability. Users cannot edit, correct, or delete individual transaction records. This is a significant operational gap.
2.5.10 Shareholder Balance Calculations Broken
Shareholder balance calculations depend on correct income source data (which is wrong — see 2.5.1) and on per-owner expense attribution. The Expense Target field (H1–H4, Com, Negu) is missing from the Expense Payments page, so expenses cannot be correctly attributed to owners.


3. Repair and Improvement Plan
The plan is organised into six phases in priority order. Phases 1 and 2 address critical data integrity issues that affect every other part of the system. Phases 3–6 deliver the new functionality described in the Andrisa requirements document and complete the application roadmap.
Each task is described in sufficient detail for an autonomous agentic system to implement it without additional clarification. File paths, table names, field names, and expected behaviours are specified precisely.

Phase 1 — Critical Data Integrity Repairs
These tasks must be completed first. All subsequent work depends on correct data.

Task 1.1 — Verify and Fix Backend Connection Mode
PROBLEM: The .env and .env.local files contain both cloud Supabase URLs and local PostgreSQL credentials. The Supabase client in src/integrations/supabase/client.ts is pointed at the cloud project neakxehuonsrlvjrxhnd.supabase.co. The API server (api/server.js) connects to the local PostgreSQL via DATABASE_URL. This split means the React frontend talks to Supabase Cloud while the API backend talks to local Docker PostgreSQL — a bifurcated architecture that causes data inconsistency.
ACTION: Decide on a single backend and enforce it throughout.
• RECOMMENDED APPROACH: Use local PostgreSQL (lanacc-db) as the single data store. Remove all Supabase Cloud calls from the frontend. Replace supabase.from(...) queries in React components and hooks with REST API calls to the local Express API (http://localhost:4000). The API already exposes routes for backup, sync, cash control, invoices, journal, reports, and sync.
• ALTERNATIVE: If Supabase Cloud is preferred, configure the local Docker DB to sync fully to cloud and remove the local API backend. This is more complex but allows Supabase Auth and RLS to function as designed.
• IMMEDIATE STEP: Run the following command to confirm which database is receiving writes: docker exec lanacc-db psql -U postgres -d landco_v2_db -c "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM income_transactions;" — and compare to the Supabase Cloud count. Whichever has more data is the current live database.

Task 1.2 — Run Journal Entry Backfill
PROBLEM: All transaction data imported before Release 1's auto-posting triggers were applied does not have journal entries. The Trial Balance is therefore incomplete and unreliable.
ACTION: Execute the backfill script against the local database:
    docker exec -it lanacc-api node /app/scripts/backfill-journal.mjs
Verify completion by running: SELECT COUNT(*) FROM journal_entries; — this should equal the total number of income, expense, bank, and petty-cash transactions. 6. Appendix — Excel Source File Structure
The following summarises the structure of the Excel files in the Landco_Accounts_2026 dataset. This information is required by the import parsers and by agents performing data verification.
6.1 Monthly Folder Structure
The Landco Accounts 2026 archive contains monthly subfolders:
• 01 JAN ACCOUNTS — 01 JAN MONTH END 2026.xlsx, 01 Petty Cash PETTY CASH 2026.xlsx
• 02 FEB ACCOUNTS — 02 FEB MONTH END 2026.xlsx, 02 Petty Cash PETTY CASH 2026.xlsx, Landco Expense Report Feb.xlsx
• 03 MARCH ACCOUNTS — 03 MARCH MONTH END 2026.xlsx, 03 Petty Cash PETTY CASH 2026.xlsx, 03 COCO EMILY PETTY CASH 2026.xlsx
• 04 APRIL ACCOUNTS — 04 MONTH END APRIL 2026.xlsx, multiple petty cash files per house (AURORA, CAJU, COCO EMILY, LANDCO)
• BDO Accounts 2026 — 01 BDO Bank Control 2026.xlsx through 04 BDO Bank Control 2026.xlsx
• Salaries Landco 2026 — 01–04 Salary sheet for Landco.xlsx plus advance salary PDFs and INSS/IRPS documents
• Shareholders 2026 — Per-shareholder monthly workbooks (Aurora, Caju Kevin, Coco Cohen, Luz Tafy) for months 01–04
• BIM Salaries 2026 — BIM salary transfer files (XLS and PDF) for each month
• Cashing up — Safe 04 April 2026.xlsx

6.2 Month End File Tabs
Each MONTH END file contains the following sheets (tabs):
• INCOME — Real owner deposit data: rows per deposit, columns per house (H1–H4), dates, descriptions, amounts in MZN and USD. This is the source for the landco_income table.
• RECON — Bank reconciliation data comparing book balance to bank statement.
• EXPENSES — Documented (official) expenses with supplier, date, description, category, amount. Source for expense_transactions (official subset).
• EXPENSES HOUSE — House-specific (personal) expenses not submitted to the AT.
• MONTHLY FOR THE YEAR — Year-to-date running totals per category, used for management reporting.

6.3 Petty Cash File Structure
Petty cash files contain multiple sheets, typically one per cash box or location. Each sheet has columns: DATE, DETAILS, CR (credit/income), DR (debit/expense), BAL (running balance). In April 2026, there are separate petty cash files per house (Aurora, Caju, Coco Emily, Landco).

6.4 BDO Bank Control File Structure
BDO Bank Control files contain bank statement data provided to BDO for official accounting. Columns typically include: date, description, reference, debit, credit, balance. One file per month covers the BDO MZN and BDO USD accounts.

6.5 Salary Sheet Structure
Salary sheets contain the full payroll calculation for one month:
• 'Folha de salarios' sheet: employee name (col C), days worked, base salary (col H), overtime, allowances, gross total, IRPS deduction, INSS employee deduction, net salary, NIB
• 'Sindicate' sheet: NIB numbers per employee used by the BIM salary transfer

6.6 BIM Salary Transfer File
BIM transfer files list the bank transfers to be made to each employee for their net salary via the BIM bank system. Columns: name (col C), NIB (col B), amount (col D).
